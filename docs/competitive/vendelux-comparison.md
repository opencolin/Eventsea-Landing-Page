## 1. The category Vendelux defined

Vendelux's framing — and it's now the industry's framing — splits event tech into two categories:

| | **Event Management** | **Event Intelligence** |
|---|---|---|
| **Question it answers** | "We're hosting an event. How do we run it?" | "Who is at the events we should attend, and how do we connect with them?" |
| **Examples** | Cvent, Luma, Bizzabo, Splash, Eventbrite, Hopin | Vendelux, 6sense (events), Hubilo (Account Intelligence Dashboard) |
| **Buyer** | Event ops / community team | Demand gen / field marketing / RevOps / CMO |
| **Pricing** | $15K–$100K+ | $30K–$200K+ |

> "Most mature B2B event programs use one tool per category because the categories are complementary, not substitutes." ([Vendelux article](https://vendelux.com/articles/best-b2b-event-intelligence-platforms-in-2026))

Vendelux's positioning sentence is the cleanest in the category: **"Most tools help you track events after the fact. Vendelux helps you win before they start."** ([vendelux.com](https://vendelux.com/))

## 2. Vendelux — full product surface

- **Founded**: 2020 (Manhattan, NY)
- **Funding**: $16.4M total across 2 rounds. $14M Series A in 2024 led by **FirstMark Capital** + **Cervin Ventures**; angels include Jonathan Weiner (founder, HLTH / ShopTalk / Money20/20) and Jay Weintraub (founder, Connectiv Events).
- **Named customers**: **PayPal, MongoDB, Calendly, T-Mobile, AdRoll, Workvivo, Coupa, Ramp, Glean, McKesson, ShipBob, SecurityMetrics, Contentful, Writer, Voltage, Rocketium, Seqera, Salient, Rain** (16+ logos on their site)

### 2.2 The 8 personas they serve

The buyer is *fragmented across departments*, and Vendelux speaks to all of them by name on their site:

1. CMOs (event-strategy + budget justification)
2. Demand-gen leaders (pipeline through events)
3. Event marketers (conference selection + execution)
4. Field marketers (regional event planning + ROI)
5. Marketing ops / RevOps (workflow + CRM integration)
6. Sales leaders / CROs (pre-event meeting booking + deal tracking)
7. Event organizers (sponsorship intelligence + attendee matching — yes, even on the supply side)

### 2.3 Feature surface (verbatim from product pages)

**Event Discovery**
- Database of **250,000+ B2B events worldwide**
- Identifies events where ICP / customers / partners / competitors will be attending or speaking
- Filterable by attendee data, roles, titles
- Replaces "anecdotal notes or last year's list"

**List Builder & ICP Management**
- Upload prospect lists or build from scratch
- Connect CRM directly for **dynamic lists** that auto-update
- Track prospects across multiple events
- Identify open deals and competitive intelligence per event

**Meetings (Pre-Event Booking) — the headline feature**
- AI-powered outreach at optimal engagement moments
- Verified attendee data integration
- **Human-led outreach coordination** (the managed-service mode)
- Pre-book conversations with high-value prospects
- Calendar populated before event begins
- Claims **6.5x engagement increase** vs standard outreach

**CRM Integration**
- Salesforce + HubSpot direct integration
- Real-time attendee-registration monitoring
- Automatic deal-context analysis
- Outreach lists pushed directly to sales teams
- Attribution + ROI measurement without manual work

**AI-Powered Intelligence**
- Automated prospect matching to events
- Intelligent outreach automation
- Deal context analysis
- Predictive attendee intelligence
- **ChatVDX** — natural-language self-serve interface (launched 2025)

**Two delivery modes** (this is critical for the Eventsea comparison)
- **Self-serve via ChatVDX** — for teams that want to run it themselves
- **Managed via the Meetings team** — concierge service, human-coordinated outreach

## 5. The strategic implications for Eventsea

### 5.1 Correction to `PRD-v2-best-of-breed.md`

The v2 PRD frames the field-marketing-intelligence wedge as "category-defining":

> *"Sponsor-side event radar — "Which events should we sponsor next quarter?" Cvent helps you run events you already booked; Eventsea helps you decide which to fund. **This is the closest thing to a new category.**"*

**This is wrong.** Vendelux defined this category in 2020, named it "Event Intelligence," wrote the SEO that explains it, raised $14M to scale it, and has 16+ named Fortune 500 customers using it daily. Eventsea is not category-defining here. Eventsea is entering an existing category as a late-mover.

The v2 PRD should be updated to acknowledge this. The PRD's strategic framing assumed white space; the strategic framing actually needs to be **"how does Eventsea differentiate in an existing category with an established Series A leader."**

### 5.2 Where Eventsea has a real seam to exploit

Reading the comparison table honestly, there are 5 places where Eventsea has a defensible angle:

1. **Per-attendee verification (V/P/U).** Vendelux predicts who will be at the event; Eventsea screens who actually registered. These are different problems. Vendelux's data quality depends on third-party sources (LinkedIn job changes, etc.); Eventsea's depends on the event's own registration list + LinkedIn slug verification. For a sponsor about to staff a booth at 7am, knowing which of the 700 registered attendees are inflated-title vs verified is more actionable than predicting which 1,500 of 80,000 conference goers might show up. **Different question, complementary answer.**

2. **Per-sponsor scoring with explicit conflict overrides.** Vendelux scores attendees against ONE ICP at a time (the customer's). Eventsea scores against multiple sponsor products in a single dashboard and applies competitor-conflict overrides automatically. For a multi-sponsor event (any hackathon), this is the only way to ship a useful dashboard — and it's exactly what BuilderShip validated.

3. **Calendar audit / past-event assessment.** Vendelux's product is forward-looking ("which events should we attend"). Eventsea's wedge (the calendar audit form on eventsea.xyz) is backward-looking + diagnostic ("how is your existing event program performing"). This is a different sales motion and a different reason for a buyer to engage — but it leads to the same downstream sale.

4. **Price + accessibility.** Vendelux's $30K-$80K/yr is gated by procurement. Eventsea's $7.5K-$25K pilot and planned $49/mo self-serve floor reaches buyers Vendelux's sales motion can't economically serve — solo DevRel teams at Series A-stage AI infra companies, university hackathon organizers, indie event hosts. **Same product category, totally different segment.**

5. **Privacy posture.** Vendelux is multi-tenant SaaS. Eventsea ships a per-event password-gated artifact. For sensitive sponsor pilots — particularly the per-attendee verified scoring that Vendelux doesn't do — the per-event artifact reads as more trustworthy and is easier to deliver as a one-off concierge engagement. This matters more for the lower-touch / self-serve segment than for the Fortune 500 enterprise where Vendelux already won.

### 5.3 Where Eventsea should NOT compete head-on

- **The 250K-event database.** Building this from zero against Vendelux's data partnerships is not winnable in a 12-month horizon. Acquire the discovery pipeline via Luma API (v1.1) + Tavily (v2) and accept that Vendelux's coverage will be 50-100x ours for a long time. Compete on depth of analysis per event, not breadth of coverage.
- **The pre-event meeting booking workflow.** Vendelux's Meetings team + ChatVDX is a 4-year head start. Don't replicate; **integrate** (or partner) if a customer needs it.
- **Fortune 500 enterprise procurement.** Vendelux is winning those. Take the long tail Vendelux can't reach instead.
- **The "Event Intelligence" SEO category.** Vendelux owns the term, ranks for it, and writes the canonical articles. Eventsea should brand around different language — "verified lead intelligence," "per-event sponsor dashboard," "BuilderShip-style audit" — not "event intelligence."

### 5.4 Concrete actions for Sprint 3 council

Per `docs/council/sprint-2-brief.md`, Sprint 3 council convenes at end of Sprint 1. This Vendelux finding adds three items to that brief:

1. **Acknowledge the category state.** Vendelux is the established leader. Update `PRD-v2-best-of-breed.md` §3 (feature pull) and §8 (success metrics) to include a head-to-head with Vendelux. Update `docs/marketing/show-hn-launch-plan.md` so the Show HN post doesn't claim "category-defining" — it's "differentiated within an existing category."
2. **Re-target the ICP.** The 4 named Sprint 1 targets (Nebius, vCluster, Tavily, LanceDB) are AI infra companies — most likely too small for Vendelux's $30K-$80K/yr motion. That's good for Eventsea. The pilot pricing ($7.5K-$25K) is at the right level for this segment. Stay there; do not chase Vendelux's PayPal-tier customers.
3. **Rebrand the wedge in outbound.** Stop saying "event radar / event intelligence" — Vendelux owns those words. Lead with **"BuilderShip-style attendee verification"** and **"per-sponsor lead dashboard"** — language Vendelux doesn't use because it's specific to a different product.

## 6. Summary in one paragraph

Eventsea is entering a category Vendelux defined in 2020, raised $14M Series A to scale, and now serves with 16+ Fortune 500 logos including PayPal, MongoDB, Calendly, and T-Mobile. The wedge that PRD-v2 framed as "category-defining" — sponsor-side event intelligence with ICP scoring + CRM integration + pre-event outreach — is the exact wedge Vendelux owns. Eventsea's defensible angles are narrower than that frame implies: per-attendee verification (V/P/U), per-sponsor multi-product scoring with conflict overrides, the calendar-audit (backward-looking) wedge that Vendelux doesn't address, and a price point (sub-$25K) Vendelux's sales motion can't economically serve. The strategic correction is to stop describing Eventsea as category-defining and start describing it as the lead-verification specialist for the segment Vendelux can't reach — AI infra DevRel teams, university hackathons, indie creator events. The 4 named Sprint 1 targets are at the right tier; the message in outbound needs to swap "event radar" (Vendelux's words) for "BuilderShip-style attendee verification" (Eventsea's words).
